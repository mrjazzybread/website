As she stepped outside and stood on the sidewalk beneath the street
lights, she tried to reflect on the choice she had just made.  She
felt as if she should feel something, but nothing seemed to stir
within her.  No joy, no relief, no real sadness either, just a vague
sense of humiliation.  She wasn't sure where it came from.  It dawned
on her that it doesn't really matter.  She was already a dead woman
and the dead have no need for introspection.  It was three in the
morning, but the street lights were so blinding that there was no
tangible difference between daytime or nighttime.  They stretched
through the entire street and hundreds of meters into the sky, as if
the legs of some great horror whose body shone as brightly as the sun.
Diana was old enough to recollect a time when they illuminated gently
instead of blindingly but could not remember when or why they were
changed.

She stood still, buried in the light for a moment.  Her shift started
in a couple of hours and her workplace was only a few minutes walk
away but in the meantime she had nothing to do.  There was nothing at
home.  All of her acquaintances were likely getting ready for their
shifts, so there was no one to chat with.  She was in the financial
district which meant that the few cafes and shops near her were
prohibitively expensive.  Even if she walked into one and wandered for
a bit, she would likely be escorted out as the staff would, rightly
so, peg her as being several social classes below the minimum
requirement for entry.  And so she wandered, half asleep, through the
city, always staying at a reasonable distance from her place of work,
as if tethered like a dog on a pole.  She was the only one in the
street, except for very specific intervals where masses of suits would
flood out of select buildings and into the aforementioned cafes and
stores, although most simply made their way into their cars.

It was half an hour before her shift started and she knew she had to
making her way back to her job.  Suddenly, as she began to turn
around, the street lights all go out.  The world descends into total
darkness, with its only light source being a modest crescent moon.
The world also becomes much more silent, as the constant buzzing noise
that emanated from the great body of light had been silenced, like a
million mosquitoes suddenly dropping dead.  The silence lead to a
great pressure being released from Diana's skull, one that she had
forgotten was even there to begin with.  Nevertheless, she was frozen
with shock as she was not aware of anything like this happening
before.  As she allowed herself to move again, she slowly tilted her
head and looked up in the sky.  What she saw paralyzed her once again:
hundreds - no, thousands of missiles in the sky, all about to rain
hellfire on her and everything she knew.  America had many enemies and
they somehow managed to knock out the power grid, penetrate the
countries' defenses and launch an attack.  She closed her eyes in fear
and squatted into a fetal position.  She wanted to live, maybe more
than ever, but that desire did not manifest itself into any concrete
action to save herself: she just stood there, tears streaming down her
face with her eyes closed as she hoped that whatever end was in store
for her would at least be painless.  But the end never came.  She
slowly allowed herself to open her eyes once again and stand straight.
She tilted her head up once again, and the missiles were all frozen in
the sky, a million tiny dots suspended in the darkness, like...

/Stars/.  With no light from below, the stars made their first
appearance in the night sky in many years - maybe decades.  As her
shock turned to awe, her paralysis remained.  She stood, mouth gaping,
staring at the heavens for quite some time.  She eventually gathered
her bearings, pointed her finger at one of beacons of light and said
"That's the North Star."  She remembered it was her father that taught
her that, a long, long time ago on a trip to the countryside.  The
night was warm and the sky was bejeweled, just like it is now.
/Night/ - it had been a long time since she had even though that word.
There was no longer any "night"; existence was just one very long
afternoon that refused to end.  She tried to remember more things
about her father, but all she could muster was a tall frame, a dark
complexion and an affable smile.  She could not remember her mother, a
realization that twisted into her heart like a knife.  Although she
couldn't remember why they were in the countryside, she remembered why
her father told her about the North Star.  He explained that over a
thousand years ago, when our ancestors were still in chains, they
would follow the North Star to find their way into a more civilized
part of the country that did not exchange flesh for profit.

She found that she was still crying.  This memory was a small piece of
a much larger whole, one that she would likely never get back.  There
were so many questions she realized she had no answers to.  What had
her mother been like.  Who were her friends in school.  What kind of a
person she was.  The humiliation she felt was now heightened as she
now fully understood it.  They robbed her of everything.  They took
her present, her past and now they were robbing her of her future.
And all with her consent.  There had been many protests, some of which
she participated, but eventually she, along with everyone else, had
been pacified and allowed themselves to be treated like ragdolls by
them.  She didn't have any particular people in mind when she thought
of "them": it was her manager, her landlord, it was the politicians,
it was the trillionaires, the child eating pedophiles who ruled the
world with a shit eating grin.  Everyone knew who they are, everyone
knew what they were doing to us, and we let them do it anyways.

As these thoughts raced through her head, the lights suddenly came
back and the sky was caked in the same awful monotone light.  The
buzzing also came back, seemingly more grating than before.  As the
veil of darkness was lifted and she began to wipe the tears off her
face, she realized that many other people had been staring at the sky
in perfect silence just like her.  She looked at her phone.  She
wasn't going to make it work on time.  She was not going to die.
